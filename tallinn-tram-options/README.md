# Tallinn new tram line options

**Question.** Tallinn has been discussing several options for a new tram line, with different routes. Which of them
would serve the most people, workplaces and services, and how do they stand in the city's strategies and plans?

**Answer (run of 26 November 2026).** Twenty-eight alignments were measured at a 500 m walk to modelled stops:
the 13 lines and variants discussed between 2019 and November 2026, routed along the real street network, and 15
further corridors of the 2019 feasibility and cost-benefit study, taken from the city's drawing of them. Six of
the 28 leave Tallinn, for Viimsi, Rae, Harku and Maardu. **No option is best on every count, and the one funded line is not among the strongest on reach.**

Score: the "balanced" weighting, out of 100. 2019 study BCR: the benefit-cost ratio the 2019 study gives the
corridor the line belongs to (tram variant, scenario "Tallinn++ with free public transport"); it is quoted, not part
of the score. What the numbers say:

- **The largest gains are in districts with no tram.** Laagna tee, drawn to the end of the study's corridor at
  Priisle, would bring 38 thousand residents newly within reach; Mustamäe 33 thousand and the Rahu tee corridor
  27 thousand. Of the study's other corridors, the Astangu branch
  (23 thousand) and Tammsaare tee (21 thousand) add the most. Today 66,400 residents (14.6 %) live within 500 m of
  a tram stop. The city has chosen trolleybuses for Laagna tee, Mustamäe and Paldiski mnt.
- **City-centre links add destinations, not residents.** Liivalaia and the Kaarli pst – Estonia pst alternative
  reach 765–898 thousand m² of workplace floor area but fewer than 5,000 new residents each.
- **Pelguranna.** The funded route reaches about 3,000 fewer residents than the route dropped in February 2026. It
  has the best strategy fit of all options and ranks 13th of 28 on the balanced score.
- **The 2019 study and this model agree in part** (rank correlation 0.74 over the 28 options). Both put Laagna tee, Mustamäe and the
  Liivalaia corridor near the top. The study rates Paldiski mnt to Haabersti (BCR 7.8), Akadeemia tee (6.5) and
  the Valukoja extension (5.2) much higher than their reach alone does here; this model rates the Astangu branch,
  Järve and the Pelguranna branch higher than the study's ratios do. The study counts time savings and traffic effects
  from a transport model; this model counts who and what is within a walk of a stop.
- **Extensions into job areas score near zero here.** Valukoja (Ülemiste City) and Peterburi tee have almost no
  residents within reach; the score is weighted towards residents and does not reward them for workplaces alone.
- **Laagna tee leads under four of the five weightings** (balanced, people first, growth and jobs, value per km);
  the plan-led weighting puts the Rahu tee corridor first. No option stays in the top five under all five.
- **The lines that leave Tallinn rank in the lower half.** The Viimsi line is 18th, Peetri and Assaku 20th,
  Tabasalu 23rd, Maardu 24th, Jüri 26th and the Tabasalu extension 27th. They are long for the number of people
  they reach: Maardu puts 10,250 residents within reach over 10.9 km, Peetri and Assaku 2,690 over 6.3 km, where
  Laagna tee reaches 45 thousand over 8.7 km. Outside the city, services and plans in process are not counted
  (see Limits), which costs such a line at most 17.5 points of 100; with full marks on both, none of the six
  would rise above 9th place.
- **The 2019 study plans a network in five stages, and a fifth of it lies outside Tallinn.** All 31 of its
  corridors (124 km, 27 km of it outside the city) are on the map by stage. 27 are compared as options above.
  Not compared: two existing railways and two short links.

`findings.md` holds the full statements with their evidence and limits.

## Method

1. **Options** are declared in `scenarios/tram-options.yaml`: ordered waypoints (existing stops, street junctions,
   one registered address, or the named end of a line in the city's corridor drawing), the streets followed,
   published figures, the 2019 study's results, a strategy rubric and the evidence. No coordinate is typed by hand.
2. **Routing.** Each line is the shortest path between its waypoints over the Overture Maps street network, where a
   street the option does not name costs eight times its length. The Laagna tee line ends where the study's
   corridor 3A ends in the city's drawing, at the east end of the street. The Rahu tee line follows the city's published
   drawing of the 2019 study corridor, because the street is not built. The fifteen corridors added from the 2019 study
   are taken from the same drawing as the city published them, in and outside Tallinn. `build_scenarios.py` stores the lines in
   `data/overrides/options/`; every run re-routes the definitions and rejects an option whose stored geometry
   disagrees.
3. **Check against the city's own drawings.** 27 of 28 lines lie at least 60 % within 75 m of a corridor the
   city has published (2019 study or planning documents); 25 are above 85 %.
4. **Stops** are modelled every 500 m. Walking distance is measured along paths from every building to the nearest
   stop, for the existing network and for each option.
5. **Residents:** the Statistics Estonia 1 km grid (2024) shared among registered dwellings by net floor area.
   Buildings come from the city's copy of the national ETAK and Building Register layer inside Tallinn and from
   the national file itself around it (41 thousand buildings, 92 thousand residents in Viimsi, Rae, Maardu, Saue,
   Harku, Jõelähtme and beyond), so a grid cell on the city boundary is shared between both sides. The estimate
   for Tallinn (456,531) is within 0.8 % of the city's register total, and correlates 0.997 with it over the 84
   sub-districts, which the model does not use.
6. **Workplaces:** net floor area of registered non-residential buildings (offices, retail, hospitality, industry,
   public buildings). **Services:** the city's lists of schools, kindergartens, higher education, hospitals,
   pharmacies, shopping centres, markets and libraries, which cover Tallinn only. **Growth:** registered homes
   planned or under construction (in and around Tallinn), and detailed plans in process (Tallinn only).
7. **Strategy fit (0–3):** place in the mobility plan SUMP 2035, stage in the 2019 feasibility study, and the city
   government's position in 2026, scored with one rubric and listed with sources per option. The study stage is
   read from the city's drawing of the study corridors, because the report shows stages on maps only.
8. **The 2019 study's own results** (final report, Tables 45, 61 and 62) are quoted per option: stage,
   infrastructure cost, and the benefit-cost ratio and net present value of the tram variant in its four scenarios.
   They are shown beside the score and never enter it.
9. **Score:** seven criteria, each as a share of the best option, under five declared weightings.

All metric work is in EPSG:3301 (L-EST97). `project.yaml` holds every source, assumption, parameter and step.

An outside comparison: the city's own note on the Pelguranna route gives 5,169 residents within 400 m of stops for
the funded route and 7,689 for the dropped one. This model gives 5,348 and 8,548 at a 400 m network walk.

## Limits

- **The lines are hypothetical.** They are a reading of public descriptions, not designed alignments, and the stops
  are placed by rule. Use the results to compare corridors, not to judge a design.
- **The score is not a cost-benefit result.** Ridership, travel time, construction cost and traffic effects are not
  modelled. Longer lines reach more people in total. Published costs are quoted per option but come from different
  years and extents, so they are not in the score.
- **The fifteen corridors added from the 2019 study** follow the city's schematic drawing and were not checked against
  the street network. Their 2026 standing was not researched beyond the city statements already reviewed.
- **The 2019 study figures are quoted, not recomputed.** They refer to the study's corridors, which can be longer
  than an option or shared by several options, and to 2019 prices and assumptions.
- **Outside Tallinn only buildings are measured.** Residents, workplaces and planned buildings come from the
  national building layer there. Public services and detailed plans in process are the city's own layers and stop
  at its boundary, so for the six lines that leave Tallinn the services criterion (10 % of the balanced score) and
  half of the growth criterion (7.5 %) are a lower bound. The tables give the share of each line inside Tallinn.
- **The Jüri, Tabasalu and Maardu lines continue lines that are not built** (Peetri, Paldiski mnt, Laagna tee)
  and are measured on their own. Four of their modelled stops stand in open land more than 150 m from any path.
- **Strategy fit is a judgement** applied to documents and news up to 24 November 2026. Both the Liivalaia and the
  Pelguranna decisions changed within the last year.
- **Growth is under-measured.** Planned buildings are counted only once they have a footprint, and plans in process
  carry no dwelling numbers. Large future districts such as the Kopli goods station are under-represented.
- **Not compared:** the study's railway services and its connecting links under 1 km are shown by stage but not
  measured or ranked. Corridors the study itself dropped are not shown.
- **Corridor codes and study figures are transcribed** from the report's maps and tables by hand; the run checks
  the join to the city's drawing, not the transcription.
- **Licence:** the Tallinn city ArcGIS services and the GTFS feed publish no licence text. This is why the run status
  is `warning`, not `validated`.

## Rerun

```bash
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
openmapstack validate project.yaml          # audit the manifest and the last run
openmapstack run project.yaml               # canonical run (about 40 s)
openmapstack verify project.yaml --rerun    # rebuild in an empty workspace and compare
```

`python pipeline.py` is the same canonical path without the CLI. It reads only `data/source/`, `data/overrides/` and
`scenarios/`; nothing is downloaded during a run. `python acquire.py` re-pulls the source snapshots and records
counts and hashes in `data/source/acquisition.json`; update the pins in `project.yaml` after a refresh. After
editing `scenarios/tram-options.yaml`, run `python build_scenarios.py` to regenerate the stored scenario geometry.
