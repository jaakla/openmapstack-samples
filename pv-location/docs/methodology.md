# Methodology and validation boundary

The reproducible project answers a bounded planning question: how much does a hypothetical PV array within the 2 km Tartu pilot produce under clear sky, recorded 2024/2025 weather, and the local DSM horizon, and what is the corresponding wholesale revenue? A browser preview runs the same source-controlled engine against the same frozen data. The polygon and user-entered equipment parameters are planning assumptions, never facts inferred from aerial imagery.

## Spatial inputs

The source DSM represents the surface, including buildings and vegetation; the DTM represents bare terrain. Both originate from the official Maa- ja Ruumiamet WCS and are sampled to the same 5 m grid in EPSG:3301. Arrays use row-major order from north to south and west to east. Original service responses and GeoTIFFs are retained under `data/source/raw`, and browser-ready grid values are a recorded transformation. Heights are metres above sea level. Cells with unusable values remain unknown rather than being assigned zero.

All metric geometry, polygon area, and horizon ray distances use Estonia's L-EST97 projected CRS, EPSG:3301. GeoJSON and map coordinates use EPSG:4326 with explicit longitude/latitude order. The initial polygon is a 30 × 12 m rectangle constructed around EPSG:3301 easting 658857.5 m, northing 6473027.5 m (26.71530398°E, 58.36880769°N), then transformed to WGS84. Its identity is `scenario-pv-001`, and it is stored separately in `data/overrides/planning-scenario.geojson` with hypothetical provenance.

The engine samples the array polygon, adds mounting height to local ground elevation, and constructs direction-dependent obstruction elevation angles from the DSM within 500 m. It samples azimuth bins every 2°, testing bin centres and edges, with half-pixel distance steps. Solar azimuth is measured clockwise from true north; a local projected north vector corrects the approximately 2.3° grid convergence in EPSG:3301. Direct-beam irradiance is attenuated according to the fraction of array samples with an unobstructed solar position; diffuse irradiance integrates the visible sky onto the tilted plane. This is a horizon model, not module-level electrical or ray-tracing simulation. It does not reproduce string mismatch, bypass diodes, tree translucency or seasonal leaf change, moving objects, snow, or current vegetation growth. The footprint does not imply clearing: buildings and vegetation retained in the source DSM remain opaque obstructions.

The WCS mosaic is a frozen download at the recorded retrieval time; catalog metadata describes acquisition campaigns in 2017–2020, while exact per-cell LiDAR dates are not exposed by the response. It is not claimed to represent the surface in either historical price/weather year. Its 5 m cells cannot resolve small chimneys or narrow trees. The normalized grid masks 658 nonpositive DSM cells and 360 physically inconsistent cells where DSM is more than 2 m below DTM; the immutable raw responses are retained. Missing cells and array positions outside supported coverage are disclosed; analysis beyond the pilot must acquire new context instead of extrapolating this grid.

ETAK building footprints are authoritative context with source-reported height attributes. Their presence does not imply PV equipment. Solar-related government thematic layers discovered for this pilot contain municipality aggregates, not installation geometry, so they cannot establish an installation inventory or a detection confusion matrix.

## Solar and energy model

Weather snapshots contain hourly GHI, DNI, DHI and air temperature from the Open-Meteo historical ERA5 service. Irradiance is in W/m². ERA5 is a gridded reanalysis estimate, not a pyranometer reading at the polygon. Requested and returned grid coordinates are preserved. Source hourly intervals use UTC interval starts; the solar position is evaluated consistently with the interval convention implemented in `src/analysis.js`.

The engine transposes direct, diffuse and ground-reflected irradiance onto the requested tilted plane, models module temperature and applies the configured system loss. Four solar midpoint samples within each hour integrate solar incidence and direct visibility while holding mean source irradiance constant; this does not reconstruct quarter-hour clouds. Installed kWp scales the estimate. The clear-sky branch uses a Haurwitz horizontal model and Erbs decomposition, while the weather branch uses the recorded irradiance. The shaded branch additionally applies local horizon losses and averages receiver power after thermal conversion. These branches form comparison estimates; clear-sky annual output is not a manufacturer's guaranteed maximum or a calibrated capacity factor. Historical weather can briefly exceed an approximate clear-sky curve through model differences and cloud enhancement. [The engine contract](../src/ENGINE.md) records the exact formulas and output definitions.

Seasonal and monthly values are sums of the hourly engine results. `weatherRetentionPct` is weather-adjusted production divided by clear-sky production; `shadingLossPct` is the local shading loss divided by shadow-free weather production. The absolute shadow loss is shadow-free weather kWh minus locally shaded kWh.

## Prices and revenue

The source market is Elering's Estonian (`EE`) day-ahead price series. Each model hour is joined by UTC timestamp. The 2025 market transition to 15-minute intervals is explicitly aggregated to hourly arithmetic means in the source snapshot; this does not resolve within-hour generation/price covariance. Negative prices remain negative. Missing prices are counted and never treated as zero revenue without disclosure.

For exported energy, revenue in euros is `export_kWh / 1000 × (price_EUR_per_MWh − sales_margin_EUR_per_MWh)`. The margin is a user assumption, initially 5 EUR/MWh. The original `shadedRevenueEur` and `shadowRevenueLossEur` remain clearly labeled **all-generation-export** comparators; actual modeled surplus sales use `exportRevenueEur`. Energy lost to shade may avoid negative-price sales; shadow value loss can therefore differ in sign from energy loss at individual times.

## Local consumption and energy value

The canonical baseline uses the explicit estimate in the planning override: 10 kWh during local daytime 07:00–23:00 and 4 kWh during the remaining night, in `Europe/Tallinn`. Day/night estimates are distributed over their local periods rather than treating Estonia's time as a fixed UTC offset. They describe a generic household scenario, not measurements, occupancy or a tariff contract. The same `src/consumption.js` applies this profile in the browser and Node pipeline.

At each matching model interval, self-use is the smaller of PV generation and demand. Grid import is demand minus self-use; surplus export is PV minus self-use. There is no battery or annual netting. Matching uses quarter-hour demand where provided; hourly modeled PV is split uniformly across its four quarters, so this cannot reconstruct actual quarter-hour cloud or appliance behavior.

Avoided import cost is self-consumed kWh × `(spot EUR/MWh + explicit import adder EUR/MWh) / 1000`. The canonical adder is zero: the initial result is spot-only value, not an actual household bill saving. VAT and grid tariffs are not supplied or inferred. A user-entered adder is a scenario assumption and must reflect the intended contract components. Negative import prices remain negative rather than being clipped.

`netBenefitEur` is avoided import cost plus surplus export revenue. Equivalently, it is the no-PV baseline import cost minus modeled net grid cost with PV. `selfConsumptionPct` is self-consumed PV divided by modeled PV; `selfSufficiencyPct` is self-consumed PV divided by demand. `weatherNetBenefitEur` applies the same demand to shadow-free weather production; `shadowNetBenefitLossEur` compares that value with the locally shaded scenario. This makes the shadow value calculation sensitive to on-site use rather than treating every lost kWh as a lost market sale.

The CSV importer accepts its documented supported consumption formats and rejects unresolved ambiguous dates, invalid energy, duplicates and unsupported interval semantics instead of silently guessing. Enefit `Tarbimine` is grid-import energy; when a pre-existing PV system or battery already served some load, this is not total household demand. `Tootmine` is observed grid export, not gross PV generation, and is not substituted for consumption. The warning remains visible when such a profile is used as a demand proxy. Historical CSV market-price columns do not replace the modeled year's pinned market series.

Exact timestamp matching preserves the supplied observation year. A calendar-profile scenario may explicitly reuse a local date/time pattern in another modeled year; that is a demand assumption, not a new historical observation. Missing intervals, leap-day differences and profile coverage remain visible. Complete-period demand and value fields remain unknown when coverage is incomplete; `covered*` fields report only the matched subset. Estimate filling occurs only after an explicit user choice and reports its imputed hours.

Uploaded consumption stays in browser memory. The public canonical project contains no household rows. A scenario export can include the normalized profile only as an explicit private-file action for reimport; raw account identifiers are not needed for modeling. PDF reports include aggregate modeled consumption and the source label, with no interval rows or account identifiers.

## PDF reports

The browser PDF button serializes the current scenario, the current analysis, the consumption interpretation, coverage and limitations. It includes the displayed map when available and labels the scenario state. Exporting it does not promote an exploratory scenario into the canonical project or establish model accuracy.

The pipeline also generates `public/data/canonical-report.pdf` with the same report builder and copies it into the delivered app. It uses the canonical run time and retained official aerial image, cropped around the hypothetical footprint and overlaid using the image's georeferenced corners. This is a source-context figure, not a fabricated app screenshot. The PDF is a document view over canonical analytical outputs; private CSV profiles never enter this published report.

## Canonical and exploratory states

`project.yaml`, the immutable source snapshots and the planning override define the canonical result. Its initial parameters are 20 kWp, south-facing azimuth 180°, tilt 35°, mounting height 2 m and system loss 14%, plus the 10/4 kWh day/night consumption estimate and zero import-price adder, with 2025 initially displayed. Both 2024 and 2025 canonical seasons are exported.

The issue explicitly requests moving and drawing arrays and estimating new shadows, and the extension requests editable household demand. Therefore the app deliberately performs browser geometry, solar and consumption calculations as project-specific extensions of the generic dashboard's measured-value preview model. These are labeled exploratory and never rewrite `project.status`, run records or the canonical findings. Numeric consumption inputs and private-file interpretation are covered by project-specific interaction tests; the generic OpenMapStack protocol checks the declared native slider/year controls. The engines are shared with the Node batch adapter; this removes duplicate analytical logic, but does not independently establish physical accuracy. Reset restores the published scenario, including its consumption settings. Exported plans retain draft/scenario provenance; accepting a public plan requires updating non-private `data/overrides` and rerunning the pipeline.

## Validation

Executed checks cover source content hashes, hourly timestamp uniqueness and expected year coverage, spatial bounds and valid polygon coordinates, nonnegative energy and ordering of shaded versus weather production, monthly-to-annual totals, exact price matching, consumption energy balance and price/value accounting, canonical demand coverage, PDF creation and declared delivery bindings. JavaScript tests use independent temporal and geometric invariants and explicit solar, demand, revenue and report fixtures. OpenMapStack validates manifest/report/run parity and performs an independent clean rerun. A warning run remains `warning`; unavailable tests remain `not_testable`.

An owner's 15-minute production CSV can be compared against the modeled time series after explicit timezone and interval conventions are checked. No owner record was supplied with this issue, so field validation is `not_testable`; the project reports no accuracy, bias, RMSE or acceptance threshold as measured. Aerial PV detection is a later phase without training labels or an evaluated model. Those missing capabilities are recorded as `not_testable`, not successful tests.
